## 1. The entry rule as built

- **v4 M4's ring levels are retired.** A level with `ring: true` throws at compile; the engine's `ringKey` and the reference's ring tie-break are gone.
- **Every level is a picture board** (`pic: true`; compile checks it): the picture inside a 1-cell frame of open ground (dirt). The **entry square** is the camp: the cells of the bottom frame row within 1 of its middle (`picture.entryHalf`, `convert.entryHalf`: 3 cells on an odd width, 2 on an even one). On the left and right frame columns a cell may be water, where a moat runs off the board. Nothing else is camp.
- **No new rule.** The entry square is the camp, so the camp rules of SPEC-v3 hold on every level as written: connected ground from the camp, BFS distance from the camp (camp cells 0, tiles = distance + 1), ties to the row nearest the camp row (the bottom row), then the lower x, then the lower y. Blocks near the bottom middle are nearest; the top is the long walk (the top border's middle of a 7 × 5 board is 9); every edge is reachable unless a moat cuts it.
- Known answers in `tools/test.js` ("bottom entry", "moat", "outline"), and a differential of the engine against the independent reference on 40 random picture boards (some with a moat cutting the board and its drawbridge, every fourth with random twists), patient and rushed, all three difficulties: 240 games, 20,583 pops, 0 differences.

## 2. Moats and gates in picture style (the choice, and why)

- **A moat is non-eatable water drawn as flat water studs** (`board.pic.water`, a light wave across each stud), in a band of rows across the whole picture and its frame columns, so it cuts the board. Its **drawbridge is the gate** (iron, SPEC-v3 §4) and the only crossing; the key is on the near side (the first moat's in a lodge in the foreground, by the entry). The row above a moat is open ground, the **bank path**, joined to the frame on both sides: once the key pops, everything beyond is reached over the bridge and round the frame.
- **Why water, not eatable water blocks with an adapted gate rule.** It keeps SPEC-v3 §4 word for word (no rule change; both critics' independent rules still diff clean), it reads at a glance as a moat and a drawbridge, and on a board where every other cell can be eaten a cut is the only thing a gate can guard. The bank path is what keeps the far side quick: without it the castle would be eaten outward from the bridge one long walk at a time.
- **A second gate** is a second moat with its own drawbridge (its key on the middle band) where the picture has room (the boss), else a portcullis (iron) in the inner gatehouse, whose key must still be eaten (v4 had such gates in Era 4 too).
- **Archer towers** are slate, each its own 4-connected group (towers never touch), standing against the frame or on a bank path, so a tower can always be reached without taking a covered block (the Hard deal must be hit-free). The archer stands on the tower's top row.

### V1 (major): castle variety

**The measure (`tools/variety.js`).** Sample each picture (frame left out) at a 10 × 14 grid of cell centres. A sample is the cell's colour, read by its palette name: water, ground, gilt, iron, or a role in its scene, so "dawn sky" is not "sky". The match between two pictures is the share of equal samples.

The names are conservative. A scene colour close to the master keeps the master's name (dawn and winter ashlar stay "ashlar"; a dawn cloud stays "cloud"), so two cells with the same colour never count as different. `--layout` reads the role without its scene: the layout alone.

### P1 (functional minor): the park limits

Said in SPEC-v4 §9 (the fix entry): `deal.park`, `deal.parkMax` and M3's no-parking-while-a-tower-stands bind the dealer's own line only. They are not checked on the tuner's output or the stored winning orders.

The stored orders are checked for winning on every difficulty and for the dead-time cap, which is what the limits protect.

In this build, the critic's `diff.mjs` finds them broken at rest along the stored orders of:
- e1-24 and e2-37 (Siege);
- g-tw-1f43c and g-met-436530 (Gallery, unchanged);
- v4-linked (debug).

That is 45 rest states. Every one of these levels wins and matches its grade, and the longest wait is its stored `maxWait`, under 15 s.
