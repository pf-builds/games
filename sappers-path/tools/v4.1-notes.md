## 1. The entry rule as built

- **v4 M4's ring levels are retired.** A level with `ring: true` throws at compile; the engine's `ringKey` and the reference's ring tie-break are gone.
- **Every level is a picture board** (`pic: true`; compile checks it): the picture inside a 1-cell frame of open ground (dirt). The **entry square** is the camp: the cells of the bottom frame row within 1 of its middle (`picture.entryHalf`, `convert.entryHalf`: 3 cells on an odd width, 2 on an even one). On the left and right frame columns a cell may be water, where a moat runs off the board. Nothing else is camp.
- **No new rule.** The entry square is the camp, so the camp rules of SPEC-v3 hold on every level as written: connected ground from the camp, BFS distance from the camp (camp cells 0, tiles = distance + 1), ties to the row nearest the camp row (the bottom row), then the lower x, then the lower y. Blocks near the bottom middle are nearest; the top is the long walk (the top border's middle of a 7 × 5 board is 9); every edge is reachable unless a moat cuts it.
- Known answers in `tools/test.js` ("bottom entry", "moat", "outline"), and a differential of the engine against the independent reference on 40 random picture boards (some with a moat cutting the board and its drawbridge, every fourth with random twists), patient and rushed, all three difficulties: 240 games, 20,583 pops, 0 differences.

## 2. Moats and gates in picture style (the choice, and why)

- **A moat is non-eatable water drawn as flat water studs** (`board.pic.water`, a light wave across each stud), in a band of rows across the whole picture and its frame columns, so it cuts the board. Its **drawbridge is the gate** (iron, SPEC-v3 §4) and the only crossing; the key is on the near side (the first moat's in a lodge in the foreground, by the entry). The row above a moat is open ground, the **bank path**, joined to the frame on both sides: once the key pops, everything beyond is reached over the bridge and round the frame.
- **Why water, not eatable water blocks with an adapted gate rule.** It keeps SPEC-v3 §4 word for word (no rule change; both critics' independent rules still diff clean), it reads at a glance as a moat and a drawbridge, and on a board where every other cell can be eaten a cut is the only thing a gate can guard. The bank path is what keeps the far side quick: without it the castle would be eaten outward from the bridge one long walk at a time.
- **A second gate** is a second moat with its own drawbridge (its key on the middle band) where the picture has room (the boss), else a portcullis (iron) in the inner gatehouse, whose key must still be eaten (v4 had such gates in Era 4 too).
- **Archer towers** are slate, each its own 4-connected group (towers never touch), standing against the frame or on a bank path, so a tower can always be reached without taking a covered block (the Hard deal must be hit-free). The archer stands on the tower's top row.

## 3. The castle pictures, per era (`tools/castle.js`)

Front view, the Gallery's style: the background is blocks (sky, clouds, the sun, grass), the castle and everything in front of the sky wear a bold black outline (one ring, 8-adjacent; the outline is a colour with cards), and the frame is the only open ground besides the bank paths. Foregrounds hold the siege camp's tents, trees and the first key's lodge. Each era builds bottom-up with a row budget so short pictures keep their sky (`skyMin`).

Colours: every level carries its own palette (`pal`, as the Gallery does), material ids by population, each id a role's colour and name (sky, cloud, grass, forest, timber, earth, thatch, roof, banner, stone, slate, ashlar, black). The role colours were searched (OKLCH families per role, then small nudges) so that every pair of roles that can stand together is ΔE00 25 or more, and 20 or more with one faded to a queue row: the smallest pair in the bake is 26.0, the smallest faded pair 21.2 (`tools/fade.js`: 0 of 15,696 faded pairs under 20 on the Siege and debug levels). Colour counts per level are bake-config `colours` (a picture needs at least 4: sky, grass, the black outline and its walls; levels 4-6 went from 3 to 4 colours, 7-9 from 4 to 5, 13-15 from 7 to 6-7).

## 4. Board sizes against v4

Patient time is set by walking, and bottom entry makes every walk longer (on the Gallery's v4 boards, about 2.5-3x the patient time with the same deal), while full pictures turn v4's grass fields into blocks. So the boards are sized to the time targets; the block counts stay close to v4's, the cells shrink. Medians over each era's generated levels (with the frame):

## 5. The dealer

`deal.parkMax` (6, new, Siege and Gallery): no squad may wait on the line at rest with more than this many sappers, and the dealer's park limit (2 squads) is now also checked after a squad's leftovers land. Without it, far from the entry square a big squad parked behind a gate turned the tap that opened the gate into a 20-30 s wait, and most Era 2 deals at the target sizes dead-ended (5 of 8 failed at 21 × 26 in a sweep; 0 of 8 with it).

## 8. The page

- **Picture rendering** (`board.js`, `B.pic`): no scenery (path, tents, trees, fields, tufts), no idle sappers, no crenellated rim on tower blocks; water cells are flat water studs with a wave; the entry square is a dark gateway with a lit rim in the frame's bottom row; the archer stands on its tower's top row (`board.archer.picLift`).
- **Runners** leave their colour's crate in the yard (a colour takes the next crate the first time it sends a sapper, as before), cross the yard to the entry square, then walk the ground as it stands to their pixel's face, and carry the block home the same way into the crate. selfTest (a Siege level and a Gallery picture) and the harness check that every live runner starts on its crate in the yard and enters through an entry cell; `SP.entry()` replaces `SP.sides()`.
- Siege levels use their palette everywhere a colour shows (the Gallery's M4 path: tiles, faded rows, spaces, chips, helmets, aria-labels).
- selfTest reads the teaching levels' lesson colours from `config.json` teach; the "only what is in reach goes" check looks before the first pop (a picture's colour opens more of itself as it pops); `SP.hitPlan(id, d)` finds a patient order ending in an archer hit (the harness's archer screen); `SP.state()` gives the board's w and h.
